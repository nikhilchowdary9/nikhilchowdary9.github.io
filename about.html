ORIGINS

I trained as an aerospace engineer before I ever wrote a line of production code. At IIT Bombay, what pulled me in wasn't any one subsystem — it was how a flight system holds together: sensors feeding controllers, controllers feeding actuators, every layer accountable to the one above it. Internships at ISRO and the Airports Authority of India, working on satellite tracking and navigation interfaces, were my first real exposure to systems where a small error compounds fast and margins matter.

Data science turned out to be the same discipline in a different material. Instead of airframes and control loops, it's pipelines, models, and decision logic — but the instinct to ask "what happens at the edges, and who's accountable when it breaks" never left.

HOW I GOT HERE

I started at Cyient Insights building predictive models for aerospace and heavy-equipment manufacturers — the kind of work where a model predicting unplanned maintenance or detecting a worn part in the field has a very physical, very real consequence if it's wrong. That grounding in high-stakes, low-tolerance prediction stuck with me.

From there I moved into causal inference and marketing attribution at Sigmoid Analytics, working across Bengaluru and Amsterdam with global consumer goods and ad-tech clients — a shift from "predict what will happen" to "prove what actually caused it," which is a much harder and more honest question. That question — causal, not just correlational — is still the one I care most about answering well.

WHAT I DO NOW

Since 2022 I've been at Tiger Analytics, now as Manager, Data Science, leading a small onshore/offshore team of data scientists and engineers. Day to day, that means being technical lead and code owner on Python and PySpark workflows, designing fraud and risk decisioning systems for enterprise clients, and — more recently — building agentic AI tooling on AWS Bedrock so that people who aren't data scientists can query high-dimensional datasets in plain language.

I went back to school part-time alongside all of this — a postgraduate program in AI/ML at UT Austin (2023–24) — mostly to make sure my instincts on deep learning and modern architectures were staying current rather than calcifying around what I learned a decade ago.

Outside of work, I'm slowly turning a lifelong fascination with flight into an actual pilot's license, I follow geopolitics more closely than is probably healthy, and I spend race weekends trackside as an F1 marshal. More on all of that on the hobbies page.
